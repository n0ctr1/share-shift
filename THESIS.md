> Latest data, stated first because it cuts against the headline: over the 30 days to 2026-11-10 Intents' share of the four swap venues was 46% (90 days: 58%), its volume fell 21% while THORChain's rose 32%, and the most recent complete week (from 2026-11-02) was 38%. The kill line in this thesis is a 90-day share below 40%; it has not been crossed, but the 30-day and weekly figures are moving toward it, and C6 already fails.

## In three lines

1. **It is the largest swap venue, at a normal price.** Over the 90 days to 2026-11-10, NEAR Intents settled $12.10B of the $20.83B that the four venues moved, a 58% share; THORChain was second with 30%. One year earlier its share was 40%. Counting one side of each swap for every venue gives 56%. Only one reading puts Intents behind: halving Intents while leaving rivals as reported (41%, THORChain first). The data is against it: the rivals' own indexers count both halves of routed swaps, and on every day where the event method applies, intents.near's events put the Intents figure at both sides too.
2. **The token sees little of it so far, but more each quarter.** Of $18.76M in gross Intents fees over the window, $5.08M (27%) reached NEAR's revenue wallets; the rest stayed with solvers and the third-party front ends that distribute Intents. Over consecutive 90-day windows the share reaching NEAR was 8%, 12%, 20%, 27% (oldest first, the last ending 2026-11-10).
3. **The one real test is not conclusive.** THORChain was halted for 37 days after an exploit. Intents grew +38% over those days and kept the higher level afterwards, equal to 41% of the flow THORChain lost. But growth of that size was ordinary for Intents over the year before (52nd percentile), so the halt shows Intents could absorb the flow, not that the halt caused the gain.

## The claim

The long-term case for NEAR rests on NEAR Intents being a business with a durable position, not only a volume number. A durable position should be visible against competitors: a leading share, kept when it is tested, at a price rivals cannot undercut, with the fees reaching the token. This repository tests those things against the three other venues that swap native assets across chains: THORChain, Chainflip, Maya Protocol.

**Result: 14 claims tested: 8 supported, 6 refuted.** The competitive position is supported by the data; the value reaching the token is not.

## Definitions

- **Peer set**: NEAR Intents, THORChain, Chainflip, Maya Protocol. All four settle swaps between native assets on different chains and have a daily USD volume series on DefiLlama.
- **Wider set**: the peer set plus Relay, Across, Stargate V2, deBridge, bridges that mostly move the same asset from one chain to another.
- **Share**: a venue's volume divided by the peer set's volume over the same days.
- **Take rate**: gross fees divided by volume, in basis points (1 bp = 0.01%).
- **Fees reaching NEAR**: DefiLlama's revenue series for NEAR Intents divided by its gross fee series.
- **Market effect / share effect**: the year-on-year change in Intents volume, split into what it would have gained at its old share and what it gained by raising its share (`metrics.shift_share`).
- **Window**: the 90 days ending 2026-11-10, unless a line says otherwise.

## Evidence for

### The stress test

THORChain halted trading after a $10.7M vault exploit and resumed on 2026-06-23. THORChain reported no volume for 37 days, 2026-05-16 to 2026-06-21. In the 37 days before, it carried $48.06M a day; while it was down, Intents rose from $56.93M to $78.57M a day, picking up 45% of the missing flow, and the four-venue total fell 20%, so the rest of that flow left the market for the duration.

After the restart THORChain came back at $21.91M a day, 46% of its level before the halt, while Intents ran at $92.45M a day, 62% above its own level before the halt. Intents did not raise its price to take the flow: its take rate was 18.1 bps before the halt and 12.4 bps during it.

How sure is this? Intents volume grew +38% in the halt against the 37 days before. Compared with every other 37-day stretch, that growth sits at the 90th percentile of the previous 180 days (median change -13%) and the 52nd percentile of the previous 365 days (median change +36%). So it was unusual for the half-year before the halt and ordinary for the year, which includes the months when Intents was still multiplying from a small base. The other venues that stayed open: Chainflip -4%, Maya Protocol +105%. There was no jump on the first day; the first week was +12%. The data is consistent with Intents taking the flow and does not prove it.

Shorter outages do not show the same effect. The data has 1 other stretch of two or more days in which THORChain fell under 10% of its usual volume; against the 14 days before each: 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-02 (2 days): Intents -3%.

DefiLlama's THORChain series was checked against THORChain's own indexer (Midgard) over 399 days: the totals differ by 0.2%, the daily correlation is 0.999, and all 38 zero-volume days appear in both (0 in only one).

### The rest

- **Leading share.** Over the 90 days to 2026-11-10, NEAR Intents settled $12.10B of the $20.83B that the four venues moved, a 58% share; THORChain was second with 30%. One year earlier its share was 40%.
- **Steadier flow.** Over the window Intents' daily volume had a standard deviation of 36% of its mean, against 129% for THORChain. On a median day Intents moved $131.39M and THORChain moved $35.66M; THORChain's ten largest days were 46% of its volume (Intents: 20%), the largest being $508.39M.
- **A growing market.** The four venues together moved $20.83B in the window against $9.15B a year earlier (+128%), so the market itself grew while Intents was gaining share.
- **Not a price war.** Intents charged 15.5 bps of volume in gross fees over the window; the median of its three rivals was 13.0 bps, and Chainflip charged 31.4 bps.

## What it means for the NEAR token

Over the 90 days from 2026-08-13, NEAR's total supply rose by 7.96M NEAR (from block-header total_supply), 2.5% a year, down from 14.27M in the same days a year earlier. In the same window 1.00M NEAR reached the buyback wallet, 13% of the new supply, and 1.45M reached all three revenue wallets, 18%. For buybacks to offset issuance, captured revenue would have to grow 5.5x at today's take rate and capture ratio.

The L1 itself is far quieter than a year ago: 741,872 transactions a day against 4,643,882 (-84%), and 71,745 active accounts a day against 291,030 (-75%). The case for the token now runs through Intents, not through L1 gas.

So the answer to "should one hold NEAR on this evidence" is not yet: the business Intents runs is leading and growing its take to the token, but today it buys back a fraction of what the protocol issues. The dated predictions below say what would have to change.

## Evidence against

- **Most fees do not reach NEAR.** Of $18.76M in gross Intents fees over the window, $5.08M (27%) reached NEAR's revenue wallets; the rest stayed with solvers and the third-party front ends that distribute Intents. Over consecutive 90-day windows the share reaching NEAR was 8%, 12%, 20%, 27% (oldest first, the last ending 2026-11-10). DefiLlama's adapter note on that revenue: "Since 2026-02-23, NEAR's captured Intents revenue is used to buy back $NEAR on the open market (not burned), returning value to holders."
- **The revenue figure checks out on chain.** Rebuilt from the chain: 2026-08-13 to 2026-11-10, the three revenue wallets received 1448846 NEAR from outside themselves, worth $5.00M at each day's price, against $5.08M in the provider's revenue series (98.5% of it). Of that inflow, 69% landed directly in the buyback wallet; the rest went to the two treasury funds first.
- **The revenue is small next to the market cap.** Annualised, NEAR's captured Intents revenue is $20.59M. The market pays a median of 22x that kind of revenue for the three rivals' tokens; at that multiple the revenue backs $452.72M, or 9.6% of NEAR's $4.72B market cap.
- **The rival is coming back.** In the last 30 days Intents volume changed by -21% against the 30 days before, while THORChain changed by +32%; every rival grew. THORChain suspended swaps again on 2026-10-31 and restored most routes around 2026-11-04 (https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/11/321170-thorchain-brings-bitcoin-and-ethereum-swaps-back-online-lifting-rune-15/); 5 of its 10 largest days in the window came on or after 2026-11-04.
- **In the wider market it is third.** Add the four large bridges and the picture changes: of $68.10B moved by all eight venues in the window, Intents carried 18%, third after Relay (35%) and Across (30%). By gross fees it ranks 2nd. Across moved $20.64B for $291.01K in fees, 0.1 bps; Intents earned $18.76M on $12.10B, 15.5 bps. Its weekly share of this wider set peaked at 43% in the week of 2025-11-17. A year earlier Intents had 15% of this wider set (Relay has no data for the start of that window, so the earlier share is slightly overstated).
- **The headline share depends on how volume is counted.** See the table below.
- **The lead swings.** Weekly share ranged from 23% to 84% over the last 26 complete weeks since 2026-04-06 (standard deviation 13 points), leaving out 5 weeks in which a major rival was not trading; the lowest week was the one starting 2026-04-20, and the most recent week (2026-11-02) stood at 38%.
- **Less than half of the growth is share gain.** Intents volume rose by $8.44B year on year: $4.68B of that (56%) is what it would have gained at its old share of a bigger market, and $3.75B (44%) came from raising its share.
- **No clean substitution in ordinary weeks.** Week-to-week changes in Intents volume and in rival volume have a correlation of +0.11 over 52 weeks: they mostly move independently, which is neither one shared tide nor a clean one-for-one substitution.

### How volume is counted

Venues do not count volume the same way. THORChain: matching daily totals of legs into and out of RUNE pairs up to 100% of its reported volume, an upper bound; reading 8,886 individual swaps, 80% of their value (90% interval 75% to 85%) went through two pools, so counting each swap once leaves 55%; Chainflip: its reported volume equals input value plus the routed USDC leg (99.6% of the indexer's two-leg sum), so counting each swap once leaves 57%; Maya Protocol: 99% of its reported volume is matched pairs of legs into and out of its own token, so counting each swap once leaves 50% (from each venue's own indexer). For NEAR Intents, DefiLlama reads it from NEAR's own Dune table dune.near.dataset_near_intents_metrics (column volume_amount_usd). Two public re-computations from intents.near events disagree on what that figure counts: one finds public swaps counted once are 46% of it (92% counting both sides), the other finds it within about 1% of public net volume before confidential intents launched. This analysis cannot settle which is right, so this analysis rebuilt it itself (below). The bridges in the wider set are taken as one-sided, as their adapters report deposits.

Rebuilt from intents.near itself: on 4 days every transaction touching the contract was listed, a seeded random sample of 4,000 a day was read, and every DIP-4 token_diff event was valued at that day's price. 2026-08-06: $89.06M both sides (90% interval $60.04M to $126.23M) against $83.87M reported; 2026-08-13: $83.98M both sides (90% interval $56.06M to $113.68M) against $69.76M reported; 2026-08-20: $115.04M both sides (90% interval $83.56M to $155.39M) against $136.22M reported; 2026-08-27: $118.59M both sides (90% interval $88.35M to $154.58M) against $133.22M reported. On average the both-sides figure is 1.00 times the reported one, and the one-side figure 0.50: the reported figure counts both sides, like THORChain's, Maya's and Chainflip's.

This agrees with the two other public re-computations rather than contradicting them. NearQuery's SQL defines its leg sum over user and solver entries as the definition that reproduces NEAR's reported volume, and halves it for net volume; soldano finds both legs at 92% of the reported figure. Their lower figures (46% once, about 35% unseen) are about flow that public token_diff events do not show after August, not about the counting basis.

The method has a limit, found while extending it. After 2026-08-31 the contract increasingly settles swaps through transfer intents instead of token_diff events (event version 0.4.3 by mid-October, no token_diff at all in a 5 November sample; data/raw/intents_event_format.json). Counted the same way, later days fall short (2026-10-15: 0.61 of reported; 2026-11-05: 0.01 of reported). Any token_diff-based count, including the residuals above, understates volume from then on, so those days are shown but not used, and the share of truly confidential flow after August is not established here.

Where the conclusion would break: counting one side for every venue, Intents would fall to 50% only if fewer than 33% of THORChain's swap value went through two pools, and would lose the lead only below 3% (Intents halved; Chainflip and Maya at their measured factors). Measured from individual swaps, the figure is 80%.

## How this can be checked

- **Every number:** `python3 build.py --offline` rebuilds this file and the dashboard from the committed snapshot, byte for byte; `python3 build.py` refetches.
- **The data, against primary sources:** THORChain, Maya and Chainflip volume against their own indexers; Intents volume against intents.near's events; captured revenue against the revenue wallets; supply against block headers. Scripts and outputs are listed in the README.
- **The computation:** The headline share was also recomputed inside a trusted execution environment: tee/share-check (SHA-256 7634f3997a76...) fetched DefiLlama's and Midgard's data by itself inside an Intel TDX enclave run by OutLayer, requested by NEAR mainnet transaction Aw6Vz41optzWmAEDvmzq7wk3YHypfhFt3pssrxPnDjk7. It returned 58.09% against 58.09% in this build, and every venue's volume to the same $0.1M; the result was delivered on chain by worker.outlayer.near with a key that OutLayer's register contract adds only after checking a TDX quote against its allowlist. What that proves is narrow: the published program, unmodified, produced the published numbers from what those APIs served at that moment. It does not prove the APIs are right; the primary-source checks do that (each venue's own indexer, intents.near's events, the revenue wallets, block-header supply). This repository checks the worker's key on chain but does not re-verify the TDX quote itself.
- **The predictions:** sealed by hash, timestamped on Bitcoin and written on NEAR mainnet; see the predictions section.

## What this analysis cannot say

- The headline peer set is four venues that swap native assets. The wider set adds four bridges that mostly move the same asset between chains; which set is the right market is a judgement, so both are shown. Fee methodologies differ between venues, and Relay's fee figure is far above the rest and has not been reconciled.
- One halt is one event. It shows what happened once when a rival stopped; it is not a law about what happens next time, and the placebo check above shows the growth could also be Intents' own trend.
- 6 other weeks are flagged because a venue's volume fell below 20% of its own prior eight-week median, with no explanation found. They stay in the data.
- Claim thresholds were set after a first look at the data and are not pre-registered. The dated predictions are sealed: their SHA-256 is in predictions/predictions.json.sha256, timestamped on Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps and tagged predictions-v1 in git, and the build refuses to run if they change.
- NEAR's market cap prices the whole L1, so the valuation line compares Intents revenue with rival multiples and is not a price target.
- Volume, fees and revenue come from DefiLlama adapters. Only THORChain's volume was checked against a primary source; an adapter error elsewhere is an error here.
